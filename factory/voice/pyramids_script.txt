Slaves didn't build the pyramids. So who did?
For centuries, movies told us it was armies of slaves. But archaeologists digging at Giza found something else: the town where the builders actually lived, right next to the pyramids.
First, the crews. About ten thousand Egyptian workers were on site at any one time. Many came in shifts for a few months, then went home, and fresh teams took their place.
Second, the food. The town had bakeries, big barracks and its own supply of meat. Every single day, cattle, sheep and goats were slaughtered to keep the workers strong.
Third, the gangs. Teams gave themselves names and painted them on the blocks, like "Friends of Khufu" and "Drunks of Menkaure". A bit of friendly rivalry, written right on the stone.
Here's the clincher. The workers were buried in their own tombs, right beside the pyramids they built. As one archaeologist put it, slaves would never have been buried so honorably.
So next time someone tells you slaves built the pyramids, now you know. It was a well-fed team of Egyptian workers, proud enough to sign their work.
