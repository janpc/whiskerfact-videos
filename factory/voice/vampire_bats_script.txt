Myth or fact: do bats drink blood? Mostly myth. The true part is weirder. Dracula made it famous. In Bram Stoker's eighteen ninety seven novel, the count can transform into a bat. Yet vampire legends came first. Naturalists later named bats after the monsters. Now, the evidence. Out of more than fourteen hundred bat species, only three living ones are known to feed on blood. Today the common vampire bat feeds mostly on livestock, like cattle. And it doesn't suck. Up close, heat sensors on its nose pick out spots where warm blood runs near the skin. Then sharp front teeth make a small cut, and it laps the blood up. Footnote: a protein in its saliva helps stop the blood from clotting. Scientists named it draculin. Here's the part movies skip. A common vampire bat that misses about two nights of meals can starve. Luckily, well-fed roostmates sometimes regurgitate blood to share with a hungry bat. So next time you see a Halloween bat, now you know. Only three living species drink blood, and they lap it, not suck it.
