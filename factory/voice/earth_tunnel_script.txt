Through an airless tunnel, falling through Earth would take about thirty-eight minutes. So where would you stop? The answer is stranger than you'd guess.
Imagine a perfect tunnel through the center: no heat, no spin, just gravity.
Reality check: our deepest hole ever goes just over twelve kilometers. The center is about six thousand four hundred kilometers down.
Jump! Gravity grabs you, and you speed up every second.
Weirdly, gravity gets stronger on the way down, peaking about nine percent higher. I checked twice, because I didn't believe it either.
Halfway, at the center, you hit top speed: in the simplest model, nearly eight kilometers per second. Satellite speed.
Then gravity pulls backward, slowing you all the way up.
After about thirty-eight minutes, you pop out on the other side of the world.
Unless you grab the edge, you fall back in, swinging back and forth, again and again.
But real tunnels have air. With air, one study found a streamlined vehicle would need about one point eight years just to reach the center.
Drag would kill the swinging. You'd creep down, bob a little, and stop at the center.
What it teaches: gravity inside a planet depends on how its mass is spread. An even Earth gives the textbook forty-two minutes. Our real, layered Earth: about thirty-eight.
So where would you stop? Dead center. Eventually.
