Cats can see in total darkness. Myth or fact? The real answer is cooler than the myth.
Verdict: myth. Even a cat needs a little light.
Blame the glow. Shine a flashlight at a cat at night, and its eyes light up like tiny lanterns.
Here's the trick. Behind the retina, cats have a mirror-like layer called the tapetum lucidum. Light that passes the light-sensing cells without being caught bounces back for a second chance.
That bounce is the glow you see. Their eyes aren't making light. They're reflecting yours.
Rods are step two. These are the cells that work in dim light, and cat retinas are packed with far more of them than ours.
Pupils help too. A cat's slit pupil can change its area about one hundred thirty-five fold: wide open in the dark, a thin slit in sunlight. Ours? About fifteen fold.
Thanks mainly to that mirror and all those rods, a cat can get by on about one-sixth of the light we need.
Zero light, though? Nothing to bounce, nothing to catch. In total darkness, a cat can't see either.
Still, getting by on one-sixth of our light? Pretty impressive.
