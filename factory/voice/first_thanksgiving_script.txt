Pop quiz: what was NOT at the first Thanksgiving?
A: deer. B: wildfowl.
C: pumpkin pie.
Comment your answer before I tell you.
Ready? It's C. No pumpkin pie.
Picture autumn, sixteen twenty-one. The Plymouth colonists had gathered their harvest, and the Wampanoag leader Massasoit arrived with about ninety men.
Together, they feasted for three days.
Deer? Real. Massasoit's men went out and brought back five deer as gifts.
Birds? Real too. Four colonists went fowling, probably for ducks and geese.
Turkey? Historians still aren't sure.
Why no pie? The colony lacked butter and wheat flour for a crust, and probably an oven to bake one.
Sugar? Likely running low too.
Pumpkins and squash? Likely on the menu, just not in a crust.
Footnote: the colonists didn't call it Thanksgiving. That label came about two centuries later.
Almost everything we know about the feast comes from one eyewitness letter, by colonist Edward Winslow.
Bonus: the menu may well have had mussels and eels too.
