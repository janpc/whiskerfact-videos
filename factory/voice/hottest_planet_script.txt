Pop quiz: what's the hottest planet in our solar system? A, Mercury. B, Venus. C, Mars. Comment your answer before I tell you. Three, two, one. It's Venus! I checked twice, because I didn't believe it. Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun, and its sunny side can reach about four hundred thirty degrees Celsius. But it has almost no atmosphere to hold heat. At night, the heat escapes into space, and the ground drops to about minus one hundred eighty degrees. Venus's air is mostly carbon dioxide. Surface pressure there is about ninety times Earth's. Sunlight that gets through warms the ground, which gives off infrared heat. Carbon dioxide traps that heat, like a blanket you can't kick off. Scientists call it an extreme greenhouse effect. The result? Over four hundred fifty degrees Celsius, almost the same day or night. That's hot enough to melt lead. Footnote: Venus also turns so slowly, one spin takes longer than its year. So next time you spot Venus, now you know. Mercury's closer, but Venus's carbon dioxide blanket makes it our solar system's hottest planet.
