Lightning vs the Sun: which is really hotter?
Three rounds. Let's settle it.
Round one, raw heat. Lightning can heat the air it passes through to about fifty thousand degrees Fahrenheit.
The Sun's visible surface? About ten thousand. So lightning runs roughly five times hotter. Point, lightning.
That heat is why lightning striking sand can fuse it into glass tubes called fulgurites.
Next round, staying power. A typical flash is over in less than a second. The Sun has been shining for billions of years. Point, Sun.
Final round, deep inside. The Sun's core tops twenty-seven million degrees Fahrenheit. Lightning can't touch that. Point, Sun.
Score: two to one, Sun. The bolt beats the Sun's surface, but not its heart.
Honestly? I'd still rather nap in a sunbeam.
