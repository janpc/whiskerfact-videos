What if you had a dog's nose for one day?
You'd wake up, and breakfast would hit you like a whole story: eggs, bacon, toast, coffee.
Scientists estimate dogs have up to 300 million smell receptors. You have about 6 million.
One researcher estimates a dog could notice a teaspoon of sugar in two Olympic pools.
Then you step outside, and the street turns into a map.
With each sniff, about 12 percent of the air detours to a special smell area at the back of the nose.
Footprints can show you which way someone walked.
One dog scientist even thinks dogs can smell time: old scents fade, fresh ones are strong.
Relative to brain size, a dog's smell center is about 40 times bigger than ours.
So to a dog, a walk is basically the daily news. Let them sniff.
Now the twist: humans aren't as bad at this as we think.
In one study, blindfolded people got down on all fours and followed a chocolate scent trail through grass.
Two-thirds made it.
