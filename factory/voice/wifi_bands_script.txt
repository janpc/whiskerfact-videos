Two point four or five gigahertz? Which classic Wi-Fi band actually wins?
Quick translation: gigahertz means the radio wave wiggles billions of times per second. Three rounds. Let's go.
Round one: speed. Up close, five gigahertz usually moves more data every second, so big downloads and video calls fly. Point to five.
Round two: range. Higher-frequency waves fade faster, and they have a harder time getting through walls and furniture.
Meanwhile, two point four usually reaches farther, often all the way to the back bedroom. Point to two point four.
Round three: crowding. In most setups, two point four has just three channels that don't overlap. Picture a three-lane highway at rush hour.
Five gigahertz has many more of those lanes, often around twenty, depending on your country and router. Point to five.
Now the weird part. Your microwave oven runs at about two point four five gigahertz. Right inside the two point four band.
Heat up leftovers, and the oven's radio noise can make nearby Wi-Fi stutter. Bluetooth and old cordless phones share that band too.
Honestly? Best plot twist in tech. Final score: five gigahertz wins, two to one.
But my take? Use both: five near the router, two point four for the far rooms.
Next time your stream freezes on two point four while the popcorn pops, check the microwave.
