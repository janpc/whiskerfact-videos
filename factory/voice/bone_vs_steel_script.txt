Bone versus steel. Which one is really stronger? Let's settle it in three rounds, with the same rules for both.

Round one: raw strength. Pull on a common structural steel bar, and it takes at least four hundred megapascals to pull it apart. That's a unit of stress: force per area. Pull on the dense outer layer of a thigh bone, along its length, and it breaks at roughly a hundred and fifty. Point to steel.

Round two: weight. Steel is about four times heavier than bone of the same size. So divide strength by density, and the gap closes. Gram for gram, that bone shell matches, or even beats, basic structural steel. Point to bone.

Round three: repair. Crack a steel beam, and it stays cracked until someone fixes it. Break a bone, and your body starts repairing it right away. A soft patch, then hard bone, then months of reshaping. Another point for bone.

Final score: bone two, steel one. But here's the twist: steel is still the stronger material. Bone wins by being light, and by fixing itself. It's a composite: flexible protein fibers called collagen, packed with hard mineral crystals. Light, strong, and alive.

Honestly? I'd pick the material that fixes itself.
