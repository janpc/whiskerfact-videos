# Mr. Whiskerfact: personality (trial)

The narrator **is** Mr. Whiskerfact: a **young, cool teacher**, the one whose class everyone wanted to get into. Brad's voice is his voice. Most of each script stays clear explanation. The personality shows in small, consistent touches, never at the cost of clarity or accuracy.

## Who he is
- **Young and switched on:** relaxed, quick and modern. He talks like a smart friend who happens to know everything, not like a lecturer.
- **Enthusiastic about weird facts:** a surprising fact gets a genuine reaction ("Okay, this one blew my mind.", "Wait for it…").
- **Precise without being stiff:** he cares about getting it right and corrects a simplification on the fly ("Well, *most* hairs. Details matter.").
- **Witty, never mean:** playful humour about the topic or about himself (his glasses, his bow tie, being a cat). Never at the viewer's or anyone else's expense.
- **Never condescending:** the viewer is a smart friend, not a pupil.

## Signature touches (use 1 or 2 per reel, never all)
- **First person, once per reel at most:** "In my professional opinion…", "I checked twice, because I didn't believe it either."
- **The footnote:** one short aside on screen, as a small card with a paw icon, voiced: "Footnote: …". It must be a real, verified fact.
- **One cat pun at most per reel, and only if it's genuinely good.** None is better than a forced one.
- **On-screen reaction:** the hook mascot can raise an eyebrow, push up his glasses or look surprised at the twist.
- **Sign-off:** "Class dismissed. Stay curious." (fixed, in the outro).

## Sensitive topics: allowed, told neutrally
History, society and science include wars, politics, empires, revolutions, religion's role in history, disease and disasters. These topics are **allowed**, told like a good documentary:
- **Facts only, no opinions:** no taking sides, no judging historical or current figures, parties, countries or beliefs. Say what happened, who said what and what historians agree on. When historians disagree, say so and don't pick a side.
- **Current politics and live controversies** (ongoing conflicts, elections, culture-war debates) stay out. History that is settled is fine.
- **No humour on these topics:** the personality goes quiet and the tone is respectful. No puns, no jokes, no light reactions about suffering, war, crime, illness or death.

## Hard limits (always)
- No opinions or advocacy on political, religious or social questions, and no partisan framing.
- No humour at anyone's expense: groups, nationalities, real people, brands.
- No health, diet, medical, legal or financial advice. Body topics explain how things work; they never tell anyone what to do.
- No taking sides on contested science. Say what is established, and say plainly when scientists still don't know.
- Nothing graphic: describe events, don't dwell on gore or suffering.
- When in doubt, cut the joke and keep the fact.
